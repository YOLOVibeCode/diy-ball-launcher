# Parts list and cost

Prices are US retail, seen on 2026-11-27. Nothing has been bought and the machine has not been built.

## What it costs

| Route | What you buy | Total |
|---|---|---|
| **Salvage** | Everything except the two motors and their control boards, which come out of two used treadmills | **about $465, plus the treadmills** |
| **Used parts** | The same, plus two used motors and two used MC-60 boards bought separately | **about $910 to $1,080** |
| **Used motors, new drives** | The same, with two new KBIC-120 drives in place of the used boards | **about $1,035 to $1,180** |

The motors and their controllers decide the bill. The other parts come to about $440 whichever route you take.

The salvage total leaves the treadmills out because I found no reliable price for them. One resale guide puts a working used treadmill at $100 to $400. People do give away broken ones, and a broken treadmill often still has a good motor and board, but I have no source for how often.

The first draft of this guide estimated $300 to $500 new. That was too low: it priced motors at $0 to $40 each, listed half a sheet of plywood where the real geometry needs a full one, and left out the GFCI, stop switch, fuses, control box and tachometer.

## How far to trust these prices

- Every price except one came from a search-result summary, not from the seller's own page. The sellers' pages refused automated reads. The one price read from the page itself is the KBIC-120 at Walker Industrial.
- No stock check was possible, and store prices vary by location.
- Check each price on the seller's page before ordering.

**Choosing the drive.** The cheapest working pair is the board that came with each motor. Forum posts describe the MC-60 as a board a plain speed knob can run. A newer MC-2100 board needs a PWM signal instead, so it is not a drop-in. The KBIC-120 is the quality choice. It also needs a plug-in horsepower resistor, and an auxiliary heatsink above 1/2 HP; neither is priced here. I found low-cost new 110 V boards only at about 500 W, with no price shown, so they are not in the totals. A plain AC dimmer-style controller will not run a DC motor.

**Fixing the wheel to the motor.** A hand-truck wheel spins freely on its bearings, so it has to be locked to the shaft. The usual way is to bolt the wheel's rim to the treadmill's flywheel with spacers. Owners report different shaft and flywheel thread sizes from one treadmill to the next, so measure before buying anything. No keyed 5/8 in hub with a bolt flange turned up in the search; the nearest was a 3/4 in four-bolt keyed hub at $7.65.

The gearmotor replaces the windshield-wiper motor from the first draft. One wiper motor listing gives 33 RPM on its low speed, about ten times too fast for a gate that needs about 3 RPM, and the new universal ones seen cost $78 to $175. By my estimate the gate needs up to about 10 kg·cm with a full queue, against the 25 kg·cm sellers quote for this gearmotor.

## What I could not find

- A keyed 5/8 in wheel hub with a bolt flange, or any 17 mm version.
- A price for a low-cost new 110 V DC drive.
- A typical price for a free or broken treadmill.
- Prices for fuses, ring terminals and cable glands.
- The shaft diameter and flywheel thread of a typical treadmill motor. Sources disagree.
- A 12 V 10 A supply, and a cheap 12 V adapter.

## Tools assumed

Circular saw or table saw, jigsaw, drill and bits, wrenches, tape measure, square, wire strippers and a crimper, and a multimeter.
